# math/01 — Formal setup

Conventions. `(X, 𝔛)` and `(Y, 𝔜)` are standard Borel spaces; `Y` is finite for
classification (`|Y| = C`). `Z = X × Y`. All densities are w.r.t. a fixed
σ-finite dominating measure `μ` on `Y` (counting measure when `Y` is finite).
`Δ_K` is the probability simplex on `K` atoms. Every claim below is labelled
**[EXACT]** (holds with no approximation under the stated hypotheses) or
**[APPROX]** (an approximation is being made), and every **[APPROX]** carries a
statement of what is assumed.

---

## 1. Objects

**(D1) Simulator family.** A measurable space `(Θ, 𝔗)` of *prior
hyperparameters*, and for each `θ ∈ Θ` a probability measure `P_θ` on `Z^ℕ`
(infinite sequences of rows). In TabPFN-type priors, `P_θ` is the law of an
i.i.d. sequence generated by a structural causal model whose structure and
noise are determined by `θ`; we do **not** need i.i.d.-ness below unless a
statement says so.

**(D2) Meta-prior.** A probability measure `π` on `(Θ, 𝔗)`. The *prior
predictive* is `P_π(·) = ∫_Θ P_θ(·) π(dθ)` on `Z^ℕ`.

**(D3) Data.** `D_{1:n} = ((x_1,y_1), …, (x_n,y_n)) ∈ Z^n`. Write
`D_{<i} = D_{1:i-1}`.

**(D4) Forecaster.** A *forecaster* is a family
`q = (q_i)_{i≥1}`, `q_i : Y × X × Z^{i-1} → [0, ∞)`, measurable, with
`∫_Y q_i(y | x, D_{<i}) μ(dy) = 1` for all `(x, D_{<i})`.
A PFN with parameters `φ` induces a forecaster `q^φ` (permutation-invariant in
`D_{<i}` for TabPFN-style architectures; permutation-invariance is **not**
required below).

**(D5) Prequential score.** For a forecaster `q`, a dataset `D_{1:n}` and an
ordering `σ ∈ S_n`,

    ℓ_σ(q; D) := Σ_{i=1}^{n} log q_i( y_{σ(i)} | x_{σ(i)}, D_{σ(<i)} ) .        (1)

`ℓ_σ` is computable from forward passes only; no labels beyond those already in
`D`, no gradients, no auxiliary model.

---

## 2. What a PFN converges to

**Theorem 1 (Bayes optimality of the PFN objective). [EXACT]**
Fix `π` and `n`. Define the population objective over forecasters
`J(q) = E_{θ∼π, D∼P_θ}[ − Σ_{i=1}^n log q_i(y_i | x_i, D_{<i}) ]`.
If `J(q) < ∞` for some `q`, then `J` is minimized, and any minimizer satisfies,
for every `i`,

    q_i(· | x, D_{<i}) = P_π( y_i ∈ · | x_i = x, D_{<i} )                        (2)

for `P_π`-almost every `(x, D_{<i})`.

*Proof.* Write `J(q) = Σ_{i=1}^n J_i(q_i)` with
`J_i(q_i) = E[ − log q_i(y_i | x_i, D_{<i}) ]`; the sum decouples because `q_i`
for distinct `i` are unconstrained relative to one another (the argument
`D_{<i}` determines `i`). Condition on `G_i := σ(x_i, D_{<i})`:
`J_i(q_i) = E[ E[ − log q_i(y_i | x_i, D_{<i}) | G_i ] ]`.
For fixed `G_i`-value, let `p` denote the conditional law of `y_i` given `G_i`.
Then `E_p[−log q] = H(p) + KL(p ‖ q) ≥ H(p)`, with equality iff `q = p`
`μ`-a.e. (Gibbs' inequality; `KL ≥ 0` with equality iff equal a.e.). Minimizing
pointwise in the `G_i`-value gives (2). ∎

**Remark 1.1 [APPROX].** Theorem 1 is a statement about the *minimizer over all
measurable forecasters*. A trained network attains it only up to
(i) approximation error of the architecture class, (ii) optimization error,
(iii) finite-sample error of the synthetic pretraining set. **Every use of
"the PFN outputs the posterior predictive" below is flagged; the central design
goal of §4 is that the main guarantee does *not* require (2).**

## 3. The prequential identity, stated correctly

Let `P` be a probability measure on `Z^n` and let `q^P` be the forecaster
`q^P_i(y | x, D_{<i}) := P(y_i = y | x_i = x, D_{<i})`.

**Theorem 2 (What `ℓ` actually equals). [EXACT]**
With the identity ordering,

    exp( ℓ_id(q^P; D) ) = P(D_{1:n}) / Π_{i=1}^{n} P( x_i | D_{<i} ).            (3)

*Proof.* Chain rule under the interleaved filtration
`… ⊂ σ(D_{<i}) ⊂ σ(D_{<i}, x_i) ⊂ σ(D_{1:i}) ⊂ …`:
`P(D_{1:n}) = Π_i P(x_i | D_{<i}) · P(y_i | x_i, D_{<i})`. Take logs and use (1). ∎

**Corollary 2.1 [EXACT].** `exp(ℓ_id(q^P; D))` is the **partial likelihood**
(Cox) of the label components under the interleaved filtration. It equals the
conditional likelihood `P(y_{1:n} | x_{1:n})` **iff**
`P(y_i | x_i, D_{<i}) = P(y_i | x_{1:n}, y_{<i})` for all `i`, i.e. iff future
covariates are conditionally uninformative about the current label.

**Warning 2.2 (this is where a natural-language argument goes wrong).**
For a TabPFN-style prior the covariate law *does* depend on `θ` (the SCM
generates features), so future covariates carry information about `θ` and
hence about `y_i`. Therefore

    ℓ_id(q^{P_θ}; D)  ≠  log P_θ( y_{1:n} | x_{1:n} )   in general,

and differences of `ℓ` across `θ` are **not** differences of log marginal
likelihood, because the denominator in (3) also depends on `θ`. Any claim of
the form "the PFN gives you the marginal likelihood of the dataset under its
prior" is false as stated. §4 avoids needing it.

**Warning 2.3 (order dependence).** `ℓ_σ` depends on `σ`. If `P` is
exchangeable and `q = q^P`, then `ℓ_σ(q^P;D)` is `σ`-invariant, since (3)'s
right side is a function of the multiset `D`. For a trained network `q^φ`,
`ℓ_σ(q^φ; D)` is **not** `σ`-invariant, and the spread over `σ` is a directly
measurable diagnostic of how far `q^φ` is from any coherent `q^P`. It must be
reported, not averaged away silently.

## 4. The construction that needs no optimality assumption

Fix a finite set `θ_1, …, θ_K ∈ Θ` and let `q^{(k)}` denote the forecaster
obtained by conditioning the trained network on `θ_k`. **No assumption is made
that `q^{(k)} = q^{P_{θ_k}}`.** Write

    ℓ_{k}(D) := ℓ_σ(q^{(k)}; D)          (fixed, declared ordering σ)            (4)

**Definition 3 (Bayesian mixture forecaster).** For `π ∈ Δ_K` define
weights `w^{(1)}_k = π_k` and, recursively,

    w^{(i+1)}_k ∝ w^{(i)}_k · q^{(k)}_i( y_{σ(i)} | x_{σ(i)}, D_{σ(<i)} ),       (5)

and the forecaster `q^{mix,π}_i(·) := Σ_k w^{(i)}_k q^{(k)}_i(·)`.
`q^{mix,π}` is a valid forecaster (a convex combination of forecasters is one).

**Theorem 3 (Mixture identity). [EXACT — no assumptions on `q^{(k)}`]**

    ℓ_σ( q^{mix,π}; D ) = log Σ_{k=1}^{K} π_k · exp( ℓ_k(D) ).                    (6)

*Proof.* Let `a^{(i)}_k := π_k Π_{j<i} q^{(k)}_j(y_{σ(j)} | ·)`, so
`w^{(i)}_k = a^{(i)}_k / Σ_j a^{(i)}_j`. Then
`q^{mix,π}_i(y_{σ(i)}) = Σ_k a^{(i)}_k q^{(k)}_i(y_{σ(i)}) / Σ_k a^{(i)}_k
 = (Σ_k a^{(i+1)}_k) / (Σ_k a^{(i)}_k)`.
The product over `i = 1..n` telescopes to
`(Σ_k a^{(n+1)}_k)/(Σ_k a^{(1)}_k) = Σ_k π_k exp(ℓ_k(D))`, since
`Σ_k a^{(1)}_k = Σ_k π_k = 1`. Take logs. ∎

**Theorem 4 (Assumption-free regret bound). [EXACT]**
For every `D`, every `π ∈ Δ_K` with `π_k > 0`, and every `k`,

    ℓ_σ( q^{mix,π}; D )  ≥  ℓ_k(D) + log π_k .                                    (7)

In particular `ℓ_σ(q^{mix,π};D) ≥ max_k [ ℓ_k(D) + log π_k ]`, so with uniform
`π` the mixture's cumulative log-loss exceeds that of the **best hyperparameter
chosen in hindsight** by at most `log K`, **uniformly over datasets, with no
assumption on the data-generating process and no assumption that the networks
are Bayes-optimal.**

*Proof.* `Σ_j π_j exp(ℓ_j) ≥ π_k exp(ℓ_k)`; apply `log` to (6). ∎

**Remark 4.1.** Theorem 4 is the load-bearing guarantee (criterion S2): it
survives Remark 1.1 entirely. This is the reason the construction is stated over
*forecasters* rather than over *posterior predictives*.

**Remark 4.2 (what Theorem 4 does not say).** It bounds regret against the best
*single* `θ_k`, not against the best mixture, and not against the truth. It says
nothing about accuracy or calibration — only cumulative log-loss along `σ`. It
is therefore necessary but far from sufficient for the empirical claim.

## 5. Fitting the meta-prior (empirical Bayes over `Θ`)

Let `D^{(1)}, …, D^{(M)}` be real datasets, `L_{mk} := exp(ℓ_k(D^{(m)}))`, and

    F(π) := Σ_{m=1}^{M} log ( Σ_{k=1}^{K} π_k L_{mk} ) = Σ_m ℓ_σ(q^{mix,π}; D^{(m)}). (8)

**Theorem 5 (Concavity). [EXACT]** `F : Δ_K → [−∞, ∞)` is concave, and strictly
concave on the affine hull of `{π : Σ_k π_k L_{mk} > 0 ∀m}` whenever the rows of
`L` span `ℝ^K`.

*Proof.* `π ↦ Σ_k π_k L_{mk}` is linear and nonnegative; `log` is concave and
nondecreasing; a concave nondecreasing function of a linear map is concave, and
sums of concave functions are concave. Strictness: the Hessian of
`−log(⟨π, L_m⟩)` is `L_m L_mᵀ / ⟨π,L_m⟩²`, positive semidefinite with kernel
`L_m^⊥`; summing over `m` gives a positive definite Hessian iff
`span{L_m} = ℝ^K`. ∎

**Corollary 5.1.** `π* = argmax_{Δ_K} F` is the nonparametric MLE of a mixing
distribution (Kiefer–Wolfowitz). It is computable by EM with the closed-form
update `π_k ← (1/M) Σ_m π_k L_{mk} / Σ_j π_j L_{mj}`, which is monotone in `F`.

**Remark 5.2 [APPROX].** `F` is a *surrogate*: by Theorem 3 it is exactly the
cumulative log-loss of the mixture forecaster on real data, but it is **not**
the log marginal likelihood of the real data under the mixed prior (Warning
2.2), and log-loss is not the target metric (accuracy/AUC/ECE). Two distinct
claims must therefore be separated in the paper and tested separately:
 (a) `π*` improves held-out **log-loss** — implied by construction + Theorem 5;
 (b) `π*` improves held-out **accuracy/calibration** — an empirical claim with
 no proof, and the one reviewers will actually care about.

**Remark 5.3 (numerical).** Work in log space: `log Σ_k π_k L_{mk}` via
log-sum-exp. `ℓ_k` is `O(n)` in magnitude, so `L_{mk}` underflows for `n > 10³`;
never materialize `L`.

## 6. Two deployment modes

**(M1) Inference-time, zero retraining.** Predict with `q^{mix,π*}`. Cost:
`K` conditional forward passes (batchable). By Theorem 3 this is exactly the
object whose loss `F` measures. By Theorem 4 it is never much worse than the
best single prior.

**(M2) Training-time, one retraining.** Resample the synthetic pretraining
distribution from `π*` and retrain a standard unconditional PFN.
**[APPROX]** M2's justification requires Theorem 1 (the retrained net
approximates the posterior predictive under `P_{π*}`), so unlike M1 it inherits
Remark 1.1. M2 is the "drop-in better TabPFN" deliverable; M1 is the one with a
guarantee. They must not be conflated.

## 7. Cost of computing `ℓ_k`, and a design consequence

Naive computation of (1) needs `n` forward passes with growing context:
`Θ(n · n²) = Θ(n³)` attention cost — infeasible for `n = 10⁴`.

**Design consequence.** If the θ-conditional PFN is trained with a **causal mask
over rows**, then all `n` terms of (1) are produced by a *single* forward pass,
giving `Θ(n²)`. This is a change to the model class (rows become ordered), and
it must be tested whether it costs accuracy relative to bidirectional context;
if it does, the fallback is to evaluate `ℓ` on a random subsample of `n' ≈ 512`
rows, which is `Θ(n'³)` and cheap. **Flag:** subsampling changes the estimand
(it is then the prequential score of a size-`n'` dataset), so the comparison
across `k` must use the *same* subsample and ordering — otherwise the
comparison is not paired and the noise will swamp the signal.

## 8. Open items to resolve before any experiment
1. Whether `argmax_k ℓ_k` recovers the generating `θ` on synthetic data
   (threat T-A). This is a test of the whole construction and is cheap.
2. Whether the trained network's `ℓ_k` is dominated by `σ`-incoherence
   (Warning 2.3). Measure the spread over `σ` and compare it to the spread
   over `k`. **If spread over `σ` ≫ spread over `k`, the method is dead.**
3. Whether conditioning on `θ` is actually used by the network (threat T-B).
4. Whether `π*` is non-degenerate (threat T-D); if `π*` is a single atom the
   contribution collapses to model selection.
